Roles and league management

Who can do what

  • Race Master: runs the league (results, grid, calendar, seasons, transfer market, members and roles, settings, saves) and can see every garage. An admin, not a driver, unless also given a driver.
  • Scorekeeper: enters and edits results, statuses, Fastest Lap, Driver of the Day, notes and AI difficulty, and submits rounds. After submitting, only the Race Master can change a round. Nothing administrative.
  • Member: views everything. With a driver: their own garage, contract talks, Team Standing and press pen.
  • Spectator: view only. Can't have a driver.

Each person has one role per league, set on the league's Players & Logins page. A driver is assigned separately, so a Race Master or Scorekeeper can also drive.

Back to top ↑

Scorekeeper

A Scorekeeper enters the results for the round that's currently open: positions, statuses, Fastest Lap, Driver of the Day, notes and AI difficulty. Edits save automatically.

When the round is done they press Submit results, check the review screen and confirm. From then on the round is locked: Scorekeepers can't change it, and only the Race Master can reopen or correct it. A Scorekeeper can also have a driver.

Back to top ↑

Race Master

The Race Master runs the league: results (including reopening and correcting submitted rounds), the grid, calendar, seasons, transfer market, members and roles, League Settings, backups and restores, incident rulings and the Activity Log.

Site Race Masters (set in Accounts) are Race Master of every league. A league always keeps at least one Race Master.

Back to top ↑

Assigned drivers

A member can be assigned one player driver. That gives them their own garage and contract talks, Team Standing, the press pen and incident reports. Any role except Spectator can have a driver, and each player driver keeps one accent colour across every page.

Back to top ↑

Joining a league

The Race Master chooses how new people get in, in League Settings. Existing members are never affected.

  • Join requests enabled: anyone with a login can ask to join from their League Library. The Race Master approves, approves with a different role, or declines each request.
  • Invite only: requests are switched off. The Race Master invites people by login name and they accept from their League Library.
  • Closed to new members: no requests, and invitations can't be accepted.

Before joining, people only see a league's name, season and join status, never its results or members.

Driver development

Form

How well you're driving this season, from 1 to 100 (50 is average). It's rebuilt from your results every time, so it reacts quickly and starts again each season.

It goes up with a better average finish and qualifying, wins, podiums, poles, fastest laps, Driver of the Day and racecraft (places gained from the grid, weighted by where you finish: P20 → P1 counts far more than P20 → P19). It goes down with DNFs.

Back to top ↑

Reputation

Your long-term name in the paddock. It builds slowly from points, wins, podiums, poles, Form and racecraft, and is locked at the end of each season and carried forward. Every rookie starts on {{ '%.0f'|format(C.ROOKIE_REPUTATION) }}.

Back to top ↑

Driver Value

The single number teams judge you on when deciding whether to make an offer. It updates after every race.

Driver Value = 0.5 × Market Score  (0.8 × Reputation + 0.2 × Form)
             + 0.3 × Form
             + 0.2 × Car-adjusted rating
             + teammate head-to-head bonus (−4 to +4)

In practice that's about 40% Reputation, 40% Form, 20% beating the car, plus the teammate bonus. The car-adjusted rating starts at 50 and moves 5 for every place you average ahead of (or behind) where your car should finish.

Each team has an entry bar from its car's rank: 86 for the fastest car down to 44 for the slowest. Clear a team's bar and it'll make you an offer when a window opens; the further above, the keener they are. My Garage shows your value broken down, and how far you are from the next team up.

Back to top ↑

Car strength

The tracker can't see the game's car stats, so it ranks the cars from how the AI drivers score for each team in your league once three rounds are done. Player drivers' points are ignored, so winning in a slow car makes you look good, not the car. Before round 3 it uses each team's car rating, which changes each winter and which the Race Master can edit in Paddock Admin to match the game.

Only the order matters, not the size of the gap: the 7th-best car is expected to finish around P13–14.

Back to top ↑

Racecraft

Racecraft rewards places gained from your grid slot, weighted by where you finish: gaining places near the front counts far more than at the back (P20 → P1 scores 19, P20 → P19 only 0.2). Places lost cost 0.4 each, because it's often the car. It feeds into Form and, a little, Reputation.

Career and contracts

Transfer market

  • Windows: the Rookie Draft when a league starts with rookies, Silly Season at half-distance, or whenever the Race Master opens one.
  • Offers are negotiations. Counter with a different status, length or pledge. Each counter uses up the team's patience (greedy asks use it twice as fast). When patience runs out they make a final offer; push past that and they walk away.
  • Approach teams yourself, up to {{ C.APPROACHES_PER_WINDOW }} per window. If you run out of options, the weakest team with a free seat offers a last-chance lifeline.
  • Contract length matters: while your deal covers next season, teams won't talk to you, unless your team releases you.
Back to top ↑

Growth pledges

There's no money in contracts. Instead you pledge how well you'll drive: an average finishing position, measured against where your car should finish. The fastest car should average about P1.5 and the slowest about P21.5. Each pledge promises to close a share of the gap between your car's expected finish and P1, so it's equally hard in any car:

A DNF or DSQ counts as last place, and from round 5 your single worst weekend is dropped. Form and Reputation aren't used for the pledge because they depend so much on the car (a slow car rarely scores points). Keep your pledge to the end of the season and the Reputation reward is added to your start for next season. Miss it and the team's mood suffers along the way.

Better status and longer deals need a bigger pledge. Promise more than a team needs and you can win an extra year, or (for experienced drivers) one step more status. Your pledge is set when you sign, and every seated driver must have one: if yours is missing (or the Race Master asks for a new one) you'll choose it before carrying on. After three rounds your targets are re-set to the car's real pace in your league.

Team relationship

Your team rates you out of 100 after every race: Delighted, Happy, Concerned, Unhappy or Seat at risk. It compares your average finish with your growth pledge, your head-to-head with your teammate, and your season goals, plus a small bonus or penalty from the press pen and team orders. Early results count for less; the judgement firms up after about a third of the season.

Fall behind and you'll get a quiet word, then a formal warning, then "seat at risk". Still at risk when Silly Season opens or the season ends and you're released: no renewal, and without a new deal you start next season as a reserve. A team that's happy with you always offers a renewal.

Season goals

Alongside your pledge the team sets goals pitched at the car: points in a number of races, a championship position, and (for No. 1 and Equal Status drivers) beating your teammate. Each goal that's on track adds {{ '%.0f'|format(C.GOAL_WEIGHT) }} to the relationship and each one behind takes {{ '%.0f'|format(C.GOAL_WEIGHT) }} away, growing as the season goes on.

Weekend targets

Before each race your team sets you one target, pitched at what your car can do, your last few results and your contract (a No. 1 is asked for a bit more, a No. 2 a bit less). For example: Finish P8 or better, Score points, Beat both Haas cars (the team just behind you on pace), or now and then Finish ahead of your teammate. It's never an order to hold anyone up.

  • Accept it with Got it on the Control Room (one click, no choices).
  • It's judged from the results: hit it for {{ '%+.0f'|format(C.TARGET_HIT) }} team standing, miss it for {{ C.TARGET_MISSED }}.
  • It doesn't count (void) if you didn't take part. A DNF or DSQ counts as a miss unless the Race Master rules it wasn't your fault.
  • If the Race Master corrects the results, the target is judged again. Hitting {{ C.TARGET_STREAKS[0] }} in a row makes the news.

The Race Master can switch weekend targets off in League Settings.

Teammate battle

A running head-to-head with whoever shares your car, on your driver page, the team page and the race page after each round: Qualifying (who qualified ahead), Race (who finished ahead; a finisher beats a DNF, and the round is skipped if neither finished) and the points gap. If you change seat mid-season you get a separate battle for each teammate. Battles between two players are marked 👥.

Beating your teammate is never punished. Leading the race battle gives a small boost to your team standing. The news picks up a new leader, five in a row, and the end-of-season verdict.

Back to top ↑

Team orders

Off unless the Race Master switches them on in League Settings. When they're on, a No. 2 driver can be told before a race to let their teammate through (more likely when the teammate is ahead in the championship). It's judged from the result: finish ahead of them and you ignored it ({{ '%+.0f'|format(C.TEAM_ORDER_IGNORED) }} relationship, and a headline); finish behind and you followed it ({{ '%+.0f'|format(C.TEAM_ORDER_OBEYED) }}). If either of you doesn't finish, the order is void.

In Advisory mode the order still shows but has no effect and makes no headlines. Switching them Off cancels any order still waiting, with no penalty. Past orders stay in history.

Round gates

Results for the next round can't go in until every player with a login has done their part: answered both post-race press questions from the last round, and accepted their weekend target. Players without a login never hold a round up. You'll see a reminder banner on every page until you've done yours, and the round page shows who it's waiting on.

  • Scorekeepers can't get past it. They can send one reminder per round.
  • The Race Master can open a round early with a note (e.g. “Carson's away, answering later”). The note stays on the round and in the Activity Log. Anything still outstanding can be done later and still counts.
  • A round that already has results is never gated, so corrections are never blocked. Nothing from before gates existed becomes a requirement.

The Race Master can switch gates, or either requirement, off in League Settings.

Race weekends

Entering results

Enter qualifying, Sprint and race positions, statuses, Fastest Lap and Driver of the Day. You can type positions, tap drivers in finishing order, or import screenshots. Everything saves automatically.

  • Save states: Saving…, Saved, Offline — changes stored on this device (sent automatically when the connection returns), Couldn't save — retry, Restored unsaved changes, and Conflict detected (you choose which values to keep).
  • Conflicts: if someone else changed the same value while you were offline, you'll see both values and choose which to keep.
  • Screenshot import is free and runs in your browser: the picture is never uploaded or sent to an AI service. Add one or more screenshots (crop, straighten or brighten them if needed), check the preview (every row is matched only against this round's drivers, and anything uncertain is marked for review), then press Apply imported results. That only fills the table; nothing is submitted, and existing values are only replaced after you confirm. If a screenshot can't be read, just enter the results by hand.
  • Completed rounds: Scorekeepers can't change them. The Race Master can edit a completed round directly; changes wait for Save corrections, which explains what gets recalculated.
  • Submitting: a review screen checks for problems first. Blocking errors (duplicate positions, missing results, unsaved edits) must be fixed. Warnings (no Fastest Lap, AI difficulty not tracked) can be accepted.
  • Only a Finished result scores. GP points 25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1; there's no Fastest Lap point.

Sprint weekends

On a Sprint weekend the Sprint has its own positions and status for every driver, scored 8-7-6-5-4-3-2-1 for the top eight finishers. The submission check makes sure Sprint results are complete before the round can be submitted.

Race times

The Race Master can set a race time for each round, in the league's time zone (shown with every time, e.g. EDT). A round shows Not scheduled, Scheduled (more than a week away), Starts in …, Starting soon (last 30 minutes), In progress for a few hours after the start (League Settings), then Results pending until someone submits it, and finally Completed. The Race Master can also mark a round Postponed. Nothing is ever completed automatically.

Back to top ↑

AI difficulty

Enter the AI level you raced on for each weekend. The recommender only suggests a change after 3–5 rounds at the same level point the same way, learns across seasons, ignores DNFs, and never moves more than one step at a time.

Back to top ↑

Press pen

After each race the press have two questions for you, based on how it went. Answers nudge your relationship a little (the total from press and team orders is capped at ±{{ '%.0f'|format(C.RELATION_EXTRA_CAP) }}) and some make headlines. It's optional; questions close when the next race is completed.

Incidents

Anyone driving can report an incident from a race weekend. The Race Master reviews it and rules: no further action, a reprimand, a warning, or a penalty (they adjust the results themselves). Rulings appear in the news and in the rivalry between the two drivers.

Extra features

League features

The Race Master switches these on or off in League Settings: race-night check-in, comments & reactions, the predictions game, the public results page and Discord posts.

Back to top ↑

Notifications

The bell shows results, offers, rulings, comments and team messages. Opening it doesn't mark anything read: use Mark all as read, and Clear read to hide old ones (they aren't deleted). You can turn on a sound, and phone or desktop alerts from the account menu.

Back to top ↑

Comments and reactions

When the Race Master switches them on, members can comment and react on race weekends and news, and vote for the fans' Driver of the Day. Spectators can read them.

Back to top ↑

Predictions

Pick pole (3 points), the winner (5), fastest lap (2) and the top player driver (2) before each race. Picks lock at the race time and are scored when the round is submitted.

Back to top ↑

Public results

The Race Master can share a read-only link with the standings, calendar and results. Anyone with the link can view it; nothing else in the league is visible, and it can be turned off at any time.

Back to top ↑

Discord

Paste a Discord channel webhook in League Settings to post race results and paddock headlines to your server automatically. Use the test button to check it works.
