Getting started

Who can do what

  • Race Master: runs the league: results (including correcting submitted rounds), the grid, calendar, seasons, transfer market, members and roles, League settings, backups, incident rulings and the Activity log.
  • Scorekeeper: opens the paddock, starts the race, enters and edits results and submits rounds. Nothing administrative.
  • Member: sees the whole league. With a player driver, also their own garage, contracts, Relationships and press.
  • Spectator: read-only. Can't have a driver or change anything.

Each person has one role per league, set on Members & roles. A player driver is assigned separately, so a Race Master or Scorekeeper can also drive. The server checks every permission; hiding a button is never the only protection.

Back to top ↑

Scorekeepers

A Scorekeeper runs race night: opens the paddock, starts the race once everyone's ready, then enters the results: positions, statuses, Fastest Lap, Driver of the Day, notes and AI difficulty. Edits save automatically. Submit results opens a review screen; after submitting, the round is locked for Scorekeepers and only a Race Master can reopen or correct it. Scorekeepers can't get past a round gate, but can send one reminder per round.

Back to top ↑

Race Masters

The site owner is Race Master of every league. A league always keeps at least one Race Master, and a Race Master can hand the league over to another member on Members & roles. Race Masters also confirm or overrule mid-season dismissals, reset a weekend, and see every change notice and who agreed to it. Pledges, season goals, team goals and weekend targets are managed on Team management (Manage menu); League settings is split into pages (League, Race weekends, Career & team, Joining & roles, Privacy & sharing, Notifications, Data & tools).

Back to top ↑

Player drivers

A member can be given one player driver: their own My Garage, Contracts & offers, Relationships, Press and My progression, and they can report incidents. Any role except Spectator can have a driver, and each player driver keeps one accent colour on every page. Players without a login never hold anything up.

Joining a league

The Race Master chooses how people join, in League settings → Joining & roles. Existing members are never affected.

  • Join requests: anyone with a login can ask from their league list; the Race Master approves (with any role) or declines.
  • Invite only: the Race Master invites a login by name; they accept from their league list.
  • Closed: no requests, and invitations can't be accepted.

Before joining, people only see a league's name, season and join setting.

My settings

Every member has My settings in each league (bottom of the menu): your role and driver, notifications for that league, your change notices, pinning or hiding the league in your list, links to your account, and leaving the league. Nothing there affects anyone else or any other league.

View modes

Race Masters and Scorekeepers can switch the display to Race Master, Scorekeeper, Driver or Spectator preview from the top bar to see what others see. A mode only changes what's shown: the server still checks your real role, and switching back restores everything. The choice is remembered per league.

Updates and change notices

After an update, a summary of what's new appears on your first page. Tick I've read these changes and I agree to them to carry on.

Separately, if an update or a Race Master action changes your driver's numbers (points, position, Reputation, Form, Driver Value or team relationship), you'll see exactly what changed, why and by how much, and agree to it before using that league again. Earlier notices stay on the Changes to your driver page, linked from My settings.

When an update changes how something is worked out, each league recalculates itself the first time it's opened after the update, and everyone sees a short note at the top of the league. The Race Master can also press Recalculate everything (League settings → Data & tools) after changing settings; they see exactly what would change first.

Race weekends

Entering results

Enter qualifying, Sprint and race positions, statuses, Fastest Lap and Driver of the Day. Type positions, tap drivers in order, or import screenshots. Everything saves automatically.

  • Save states: Saving…, Saved, Offline (stored on this device and sent when you're back), Couldn't save (retry), Restored, Conflict (choose which values to keep).
  • Screenshot import is free and runs in your browser: the picture is never uploaded or sent to an AI service. Check the preview, then Apply imported results; it only fills the table.
  • Submitting: the review blocks problems (duplicate positions, missing results, unsaved edits) and warns about the rest (no Fastest Lap, AI difficulty not tracked).
  • Completed rounds: only a Race Master can change them, with Save corrections, which explains what's recalculated.
Back to top ↑

Result statuses

Finished: classified; scores for the position. DNF: retired; no points, still a start. DNS: didn't start; no points, not a start. DSQ: disqualified; no points. Not Run: nothing entered yet. Sprint and Grand Prix statuses are separate.

Race times

The Race Master can set a time for each round, in the league's time zone. A round shows Not scheduled, Scheduled, Starts in…, Starting soon, In progress (for a few hours, set in League settings → Race weekends), Results pending and Completed, or Postponed. Nothing is completed automatically. The calendar can be added to your own calendar app (.ics).

Round gates

The next race can't start until every player with a login has done their part: answered the post-race press from the round before, answered the pre-race press, and chosen their weekend target (whichever the Race Master switched on, in League settings → Race weekends). A reminder banner shows what's waiting on you. Rounds further ahead just say they aren't next yet. (With race weekends off, the same checklist holds back results for the next round instead.)

  • Scorekeepers can't get past a gate; the Race Master can start the race anyway with a note (kept on the round and in the Activity log). Anything outstanding still counts when done later, except the pre-race press, which closes at lights out.
  • A round that already has results is never gated, so corrections are never blocked.

AI difficulty

Enter the AI level (0–110) you raced on each weekend. After every completed round the recommendation looks at recent tracked rounds, at any level, with the latest counting most. Each player driver's result is read two ways:

  • Against the car: where that car usually finishes, the points that finish is worth, and their AI teammate. Finishing where your car should is "about right".
  • Against the whole grid: the same, measured from the middle of the grid, whatever the car. Finishing near the back always reads as struggling.

By default a round counts half of each, so a slow car is taken into account but being at the back still brings the level down, leaving room for a breakout season. The Race Master can switch to car only or whole grid only (League settings → Race weekends).

  • Everyone too good or everyone struggling: it moves several levels. One clear round is enough to move it.
  • Mixed (one struggling, another fine): it moves half as far, toward the one who's struggling.
  • Never up while someone is struggling or near the back of the grid (about P16 or worse): it holds instead.
  • Never more than {{ C.DIFF_MAX_STEP }} at once.

Each recommendation shows the game's level band: {% for lo, hi, name in C.DIFF_BANDS %}{{ name }} {{ lo }}–{{ hi }}{{ ', ' if not loop.last else '' }}{% endfor %}. Most players are comfortable in the low 80s. A one-line summary underneath says which rounds were used, which weren't and why, and whether Sprint points counted.

DNFs and rounds marked Don't track don't count. Sprint points count unless the league turns that off. It's a suggestion: the Race Master picks the level.

The race weekend

Every round goes through four stages (League settings → Race weekends, on unless the Race Master turns it off):

  1. Upcoming: on the calendar. Predictions, check-ins and weekend targets as usual; results can't go in yet.
  2. Paddock open: opens by itself an hour before the scheduled race time (the next time anyone opens the league), or when a Scorekeeper or the Race Master presses Open the paddock. Drivers answer two pre-race press questions, choose their weekend target and check in. The round page shows who's ready.
  3. Lights out: a Scorekeeper or the Race Master presses Start the race. With round gates on, everyone has to be ready first; only the Race Master can start anyway, with a note. Predictions and pre-race press close. Results can go in from now on.
  4. Chequered flag: the results are submitted. Post-race press, the race summary and headlines follow.

Only the next round can open, and only once every player driver has a login linked (or the Race Master ticked No account for them on Members & roles). Alerts for the paddock opening and lights out are under Race day in your notification settings.

Reset weekend (Race Master, on the round page): puts the latest round back to Upcoming, as if the weekend never started. Its results, pre- and post-race press, weekend targets, predictions, check-ins, incidents and headlines are removed and every effect on team relationships is undone. A confirmation page lists everything first, a backup is saved, and each driver affected sees a change notice.

Press

Before the race (while the paddock is open) the press ask two questions picked from your situation: your last result, the battle with your teammate, your weekend target, a Sprint weekend, a points drought, the title fight, a rival close by in the standings, the transfer window and more. After the race they ask about what happened: a win or podium, places gained or lost from qualifying, pole, the fastest lap, Driver of the Day, the Sprint, a retirement (or another one), first points or a drought, your teammate and your target.

What you say gets back to your team (a small nudge to the relationship, capped at ±{{ '%.0f'|format(C.RELATION_EXTRA_CAP) }} with other extras) and some answers make headlines. Open questions and everything you've said are on the Press page. With round gates on, pre-race answers are needed before lights out and post-race answers before the next race starts.

Incidents

Anyone driving can report an incident from a race weekend. The Race Master rules: no further action, a reprimand, a warning or a penalty (they adjust the results themselves). Rulings appear in the news and in the rivalry between the drivers.

Your driver

Form

How well you're driving this season, 1–100 (50 is average). It's rebuilt from your results each time and starts again each season: better finishes and qualifying, wins, podiums, poles, fastest laps, Driver of the Day and racecraft raise it; DNFs lower it.

Back to top ↑

Reputation

Your long-term name in the paddock. It builds slowly from points, wins, podiums, poles, Form and racecraft, is locked at the end of each season and carried forward. Rookies start on {{ '%.0f'|format(C.ROOKIE_REPUTATION) }}. Kept pledges and met team goals add to next season's starting Reputation.

Back to top ↑

Racecraft

Places gained from your grid slot, weighted by where you finish: P20 → P1 scores 19, P20 → P19 only 0.2. Places lost cost 0.4 each, because it's often the car. It feeds into Form and, a little, Reputation.

Back to top ↑

Driver Value

The number teams judge you on when deciding whether to make an offer. It updates after every race.

Driver Value = 0.5 × Market Score  (0.8 × Reputation + 0.2 × Form)
               + 0.3 × Form
               + 0.2 × Car-adjusted rating
               + teammate head-to-head bonus (−4 to +4)

About 40% Reputation, 40% Form and 20% beating the car. The car-adjusted rating starts at 50 and moves 5 for every place you average ahead of (or behind) where your car should finish. Each team has an entry bar from its car's rank (86 for the fastest car down to 44 for the slowest); clear it and they'll make you an offer when a window opens. My Garage shows the breakdown and the next team up.

Back to top ↑

Car strength

Cars are ranked from how the AI drivers score for each team in your league once three rounds are done (player drivers' points are ignored, so winning in a slow car makes you look good, not the car). Before that it uses each team's car rating, which changes each winter and which the Race Master can edit in Drivers & teams. Only the order matters.

Statistics

Statistics shows the season from submitted rounds: points progression, finish distribution, qualifying vs race, Sprint results, teammate head-to-heads, reliability and player drivers season over season. Player drivers only hides the AI field.

Rivalries

Compare any two drivers: the race head-to-head over time, the season's points side by side and incidents between them.

Teammate battle

A running head-to-head with whoever shares your car: qualifying, race (a finisher beats a DNF; skipped if neither finished) and the points gap, with a separate battle for each teammate if you change seat. Battles between two players are marked 👥. Beating your teammate is never punished, and leading the race battle gives a small boost to your team relationship.

Contracts and your team

Transfer market

  • Windows: the Rookie Draft when a league starts with rookies, Silly Season at half-distance, or whenever the Race Master opens one.
  • Offers are negotiations: counter with a different status, length or pledge. Each counter uses up the team's patience (greedy asks twice as fast); then comes a final offer, and pushing past it ends talks.
  • Approach teams yourself, up to {{ C.APPROACHES_PER_WINDOW }} per window, with a message or an interview. If you run out of options, the weakest team with a free seat offers a last-chance deal.
  • While your contract covers next season, other teams won't talk to you unless your team releases you.

Everything is on Contracts & offers.

Talking to teams

Teams read what you write. They notice what you're offering (commitment, development, results, being a team player, respect, your record) and how you say it: arrogance, blaming the car or team and shouting all count against you. Each team wants something different, based on where its car is plus one thing it cares about most, so it pays to learn what each team likes. They tell you what they took from your message.

An interview is four questions and a goal you set with the team, which they check against your last season (a goal your results don't back up looks like a bluff). It uses one approach and can matter a little more than a message.

A good message or interview can win over a team that's on the fence, or keep them talking longer in a negotiation. It can't make a team sign someone far below their bar, and top teams still won't sign rookies. Everything is worked out on the site itself: no AI service reads your messages.

Contracts and seats

Grid & contracts lists every contract as current, upcoming, expiring, expired, historical or seated elsewhere, and each seat as confirmed, provisional, temporary or with no contract. At the end of a season the rollover review asks what happens to each player driver whose contract ends (renew, keep provisionally or release), after a backup.

Team relationship

Your team rates you out of 100 after every race: Delighted, Happy, Concerned, Unhappy or Seat at risk, from your average finish against your pledge, your teammate head-to-head and your season goals, plus small extras from press answers and weekend targets. Early results count for less.

Fall behind and you get a quiet word, then a formal warning, then "Seat at risk". Still at risk when Silly Season opens or the season ends and you're released. It's all on Relationships.

Back to top ↑

Final warnings and mid-season dismissals

If you're at "Seat at risk" after four or more rounds (with at least two left), your team can give you a final warning: one race, one target pitched at the car, for example Finish P14 or better at Monza, or you're out.

  • Met: your seat is safe for now and the warning eases.
  • DNF or DNS: it doesn't count either way, and the same demand applies to the next race.
  • Missed: the Race Master confirms the dismissal or overrules it with a note. If you're dropped, the best available reserve takes the seat for the rest of the season; your results, points and Reputation stay yours and you're free to talk to other teams.

One decision per driver per season. The Race Master can switch this off in League settings → Career & team.

Season goals

Alongside your pledge the team sets goals pitched at the car: points in a number of races, a championship position, and (for No. 1 and Equal Status drivers) beating your teammate. Each goal on track adds {{ '%.0f'|format(C.GOAL_WEIGHT) }} to the relationship and each one behind takes {{ '%.0f'|format(C.GOAL_WEIGHT) }} away. A Race Master can re-issue season goals for one driver or everyone (Team management); any change to your numbers is shown to you first.

Weekend targets

Before each race your team offers you three targets, pitched at your car, your recent results and your role, and you choose one and lock it in (on the round page or the Control Room). You can't change it afterwards.

For example Finish P12 or better, Finish P9 or better or Finish ahead of your teammate. If you haven't chosen by lights out (or you don't have a login) you race for the Standard one. It's void if you don't take part. A DNF or DSQ counts as a miss unless the Race Master rules it wasn't your fault. The Race Master can let you choose again or offer new targets before the race, or remove a target on any round (removing one after the race undoes its effect, and you'll see a change notice).

Team goals (Safe, Competitive, Ambitious)

When the league turns them on, each team with a player driver picks one Constructors' goal before round 1: Safe (+1 Reputation if met, nothing if missed), Competitive (+3 / −1) or Ambitious (+6 / −3). Targets come from car strength, last season's Constructors' position and scoring, the lineup, the number of rounds and Sprint weekends. A goal chosen after the season has started counts the points already scored and only predicts the rounds left, using how the team has actually been scoring (it counts more with every round). Safe always needs more than you have now, and each level asks for clearly more than the one below. Met means finishing at or above the target position or scoring the target points; teams level on points count as the lower place. Choices lock when round 1 is submitted; a Race Master can re-push the targets or reset a team's goal so they choose again (Team management). While your team's choice is open you'll be asked to make it before anything else, and re-pushed targets are shown to you to agree to. The reward or penalty is added to next season's starting Reputation.

Team orders

Off unless the Race Master turns them on in League settings → Career & team. When On, a No. 2 driver can be told before a race to let their teammate through: finish ahead and you ignored it ({{ '%+.0f'|format(C.TEAM_ORDER_IGNORED) }} relationship and a headline), finish behind and you followed it ({{ '%+.0f'|format(C.TEAM_ORDER_OBEYED) }}). Advisory shows orders with no effect.

Turning them Off removes every team order, including past ones, and undoes any effect they had; each driver affected sees a change notice.

League extras

League features

The Race Master switches these on or off in League settings → Career & team (extras) and Privacy & sharing: race-night check-in, comments and reactions, predictions, the public results page and Discord posts.

Announcements

Race Masters post announcements that appear at the top of the Control Room: pinned, aimed at some roles, scheduled for later or with a time to come down. The form shows how many members will see it, get a phone alert and get an email; email only goes to members who want Announcements email from that league.

Notifications

The bell shows results, offers, rulings, comments and team messages. Opening it doesn't mark anything read: use Mark all as read, and Clear read to hide old ones.

Every league has its own settings. In a league, open My settings → Choose notifications to pick email and phone alerts by category, use a preset or mute that league. Every email names its league and links to change or unsubscribe. Pause every email in your account stops all league emails at once. Race Masters can see what was sent (never to whom) under Manage → Notifications.

Admin and account

Backups and recovery

A copy of the league is saved automatically every few hours, and always before a season rollover, a restore, an upgrade or deleting the league. Backups & data lists them; restoring one backs up the current state first, so a restore can be undone. Importing a .f1career file shows what's in it first and always creates a new league.

Accounts and the site owner

Everyone creates their own account from Sign up on the login page, then joins leagues by invitation or by asking. There's no list of accounts anywhere on the site.

The site owner is the one account that runs the site. It's made on first start with a setup code from the host's settings (on Render: your service → Environment → F1_TRACKER_SETUP_CODE). The owner is Race Master of every league, controls site settings, and is the only one who can help someone who's lost their account: look it up by exact username or email (every account using that email is listed), then set a new password, turn off two-step sign-in, sign it out everywhere or delete it.

New passwords need at least 8 characters and can't be a common password. Length matters more than symbols; a password manager is fine.

Forgot your password? Use Forgot password? on the login page if your account has an email; otherwise ask the site owner.

Back to top ↑

Account security

Your account lists the devices you're signed in on; end any of them, or all the others at once. Changing your password signs out every other device. Two-step sign-in adds a 6-digit code from an authenticator app; if you lose your phone, the site owner can switch it off.

Your data, leaving and handing over

Download everything your account holds from Account → Your data. Leave a league from its My settings page (type its name to confirm); your driver and results stay. Race Masters can hand a league over on Members & roles. Deleting your account removes your login; leagues keep their results. The only Race Master of a league has to hand over first.
